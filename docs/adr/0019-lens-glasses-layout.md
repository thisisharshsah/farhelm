# 0019: Glasses layout: app bar, profile, player overlay and watch history

Status: accepted, 2026-11-18. User requests that day, after [ADR 0018](0018-lens-voice.md): top and bottom bars that stay put, apps changed with left and right, no chat on the glasses, a profile for every setting, Apple-like switches and motion, song-aware recommendations, controls over the video, watch history, hold to seek; then, after trying it, focus that stays on the app bar, YouTube laid out as on an Android TV with every video's details, and components of glass with water-like reflections, as in Companion and QuantNepal; then earlier searches right under the search bar, no banner for the video in focus, and the same details on every card; then recommendations that keep coming as you scroll, on YouTube and under the video playing. **Checked locally**: unit and API tests, and the end-to-end run with Chromium's spatial navigation on and YouTube's player stubbed. Not yet checked on the glasses.

## Context

The glasses showed a home grid and a chat thread. On a 600 x 600 additive display, a thread spends the screen on what was said instead of what came back, and a grid takes a pinch to leave every app. The display adds light: black is see-through, every lit pixel glares and costs battery, so fills behind content are cost without information. YouTube removed `relatedToVideoId` from search in August 2023, so there is no "related videos" call to build Up next on.

## Decision

- **Frame.** A top bar (profile avatar, clock, Meta AI and battery) and an app bar (YouTube, Companion, Notes, Timer, the account's websites, then "+") float over the app, which runs full screen and scrolls under them. Swiping left or right on the bar changes app; the selected one becomes a drop with its name. Only the selected app can take focus (roving tabindex), so focus coming down from a page never lands on a neighbour. After a swipe on the bar, focus is held there until the next key or pointer press: whatever takes it meanwhile (the glasses' own focus moving too, an app taking focus as it opens) gives it back. Apps replace history entries; the player and the profile are pushed, so Back closes them (`src/lens/lib/router.ts`).
- **No chat on the glasses.** Requests from the phone, Meta AI or the glasses' own bars still go through the chat API, and the glasses show their result: results open YouTube, a note opens Notes, a timer starts and shows, a problem is a short notice (`features/sync/effects.ts`). The phone keeps its chat.
- **Profile.** One list: 24-hour clock, reduce motion, autoplay next, numbers for voice, clear watch or search history (two pinches), websites, Companion and voice status, sign out. Settings belong to the glasses (`localStorage`); history belongs to the account.
- **Liquid glass.** Components are mostly clear, after Companion's `GlassBorder` and QuantNepal's liquid glass: a sheen falling from the top, a rim brightest top left and bottom right, and a glow at the foot as in a drop of water. What floats (the app bar, the controls over the video, notices) bends whatever passes behind it into its rim through an SVG displacement lens on the backdrop (`src/lens/ui/Glass.tsx`, `lensField.ts`, ported from QuantNepal's `liquidDrop.ts`); the app bar's splits the colours a little at the rim. Focus lights the rim white and runs a ripple of light across the glass once. Lens's own app icons are tinted glyphs and YouTube shows its own mark; website icons keep a light tile, since many favicons are dark on transparent. Backdrop filters are blur only: Chromium leaves colour filters on a backdrop unclipped, a square around a round button.
- **YouTube as on a TV.** The search bar, earlier searches as words right under it, then rows to move along with left and right: the latest results, Watched, For you. No banner for the video in focus: every card carries its own details (length on the picture, then title, channel and age, views and likes), and the player shows the description. Search and Up next answers carry details from the one `videos.list` call already made for lengths (`part=contentDetails,statistics,snippet`, still 1 unit), with 320 x 180 thumbnails; the edge cache keys moved to v2 so older answers without details are not served. Videos that still come without them (watch history stores none; chat answers saved before) get them by id: `GET /api/youtube/details?ids=` answers up to 50 in one `videos.list` call, each video cached at the edge for six hours (`worker/media/details.ts`); the glasses send what a screen lacks as one request and keep answers for the session (`features/youtube/details.ts`). New results take focus only when asked for in the last two minutes and the wearer is not on a bar.
- **Player.** Full screen, controls over the picture as liquid glass: replay, back 10, play or pause, forward 10, next, speed. Below the picture, its details and an Up next row. They fade while playing and the first swipe only brings them back. Back and forward keep seeking while held, faster the longer the hold and the longer the video (`features/youtube/hold.ts`); a held key's repeats drive the steps, so a platform that never sends repeats or a key up gets one clean step, never a runaway. At the end, the next video counts down unless the wearer acts.
- **Recommendations that keep coming.** Rows are named by a key and answered a page of 50 at a time (`worker/media/rows.ts`): `related:<video>` (more like it: for music, category 10, the artist from an "Artist - Song" title, else the channel; otherwise the video's tags or title), `channel:<id>` (its uploads, through `playlistItems.list`), `popular:<category>` or `popular:all` (`videos.list chart=mostPopular` in the person's country), and `search:<query>`. `GET /api/youtube/rows?seed=` answers the first page of a video's related, channel and popular rows at once; `GET /api/youtube/row?key=&page=` answers the next page of any row. Pages, a video's facts (7 days) and a channel's uploads list (30 days) are cached at the edge. On the glasses (`features/youtube/rows.ts`, `Feed.tsx`, `Shelf.tsx`) a row shows 12 cards and adds 12 as focus comes within 4 of the end, asking for the next page before it is needed; a feed adds the next source's rows as focus reaches its last two rows. Sources: the video playing (in the player), the 8 watched last, then popular now; a row two sources share shows once. YouTube's page labels them For you (after the video played last), More from, Popular in, Because you watched and Popular now; the player's first row is Up next. Chat searches ask for 50 and keep 12 in the answer, so the results row grows from the same cached page. A swipe up from the app bar returns to where the wearer was in the app, else its main item, rather than to whatever sits just above the bar. `GET /api/youtube/next` is gone.
- **Watch history.** `watch_history` (migration 0003): one row per account and video, newest first, kept to 200, older than a year removed by the daily cleanup. `GET /api/history`, `PUT /api/history/:id` (the thumbnail comes from the id, never the request), `DELETE` one or all. The player saves when a video really starts, every 30 seconds while it plays, on pause, at the end and when it closes (`keepalive`), and resumes from the saved position unless that was the first 15 seconds or the last 20. The glasses list it on YouTube with a watched bar; the console lists it under Apps, YouTube, where any video plays again on the glasses.

## Consequences

- Focus left the app bar on the glasses when changing app. In Chromium with spatial navigation on, the bar's own handling (which prevents the default) keeps it; the hold above makes it stay whatever the glasses' focus or an app does, and the end-to-end run checks both a jump during the key press and one just after it.

- Fixed on the way: a cached icon, search or Up next answer came back from the Cache API with immutable headers, so the security-headers middleware failed the second request for the same icon with a 500. Cached answers are now copied first (`worker/lib/cache.ts`), with tests.
- Hold to seek depends on how the Neural Band reports a held pinch. Meta documents arrow keys and Enter, not key repeat or long press; where none arrive, a press still skips 10 seconds. Meta's opt-in continuous drag (pointer events) could carry a hold later.
- Up next is by artist or topic, not YouTube's own related list. Each page of it costs about 101 units on a miss, so the feed follows at most 8 videos: scrolling through all of it the first time costs about 900 of the 10,000 a day, and nothing again for 12 hours.
- History rows keep no views or likes, which would go stale; they are filled in when shown, so a screen of history costs at most 1 unit, and nothing within six hours.
- Not done: no thumbnails from history on the phone's home, and no per-glasses history.
