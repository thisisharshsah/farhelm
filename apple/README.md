# Companion

A native macOS conversation copilot. It listens to both sides of a conversation (your mic and system audio), transcribes locally with Parakeet, and drafts what you should say next using details you provide. It can also read your screen and answer the question shown there. Everything lives in a glass overlay pinned below the camera. By default it has no menu bar icon and no Dock icon; Settings can show either.

Plan: Companion works on its own, with the person's own model keys; Farhelm is an optional engine it can connect to, and Orbit is retired once its safety ideas are here. See [ADR 0013](../docs/adr/0013-farhelm-companion-orbit.md).

## Features

- Live transcription, fully on-device (Parakeet v3 via FluidAudio, CoreML on the Neural Engine)
- Two streams: mic = You, system audio = Them (Zoom, Meet, any app). Same-speaker lines merge into paragraphs, filler-only sounds are dropped, and long speech is cut at a pause. Settings > Listening has an English-only model (Parakeet v2) that is more accurate when the conversation is in English
- Transcript polish (on by default, Settings > Replies): once a speaker's turn settles, the model re-reads it in context and rewrites it for readability, fixing misheard words using your details and attached files as vocabulary. It may not add, drop or answer anything. Polished lines carry a wand; click it to see what was originally heard. The same pass re-checks who said each mic line from context (who asked, who answered, what you would know) and corrects a clearly wrong label, marked with a swap icon; system-audio lines are always Them and never relabeled. Replies are drafted from the polished text
- Context that lasts: before transcript lines leave the recent window (30 lines) they are folded into running notes that travel with every request, so a long conversation keeps its beginning. The notes are saved with the conversation. The Notes chip opens them at any time, with a button to summarize everything up to now on demand. Automatic replies and screen answers also see the answers already given, so they stay consistent and don't repeat themselves
- No repeated work: a question that was already answered (asked again, or the same lines coming back relabeled or rewritten) reuses the answer in the thread instead of generating it again; Recheck only goes through lines it has not checked yet (pressing it with nothing new redoes everything); the automatic write-up is skipped when the transcript has not changed
- Call wrap-up: the notes below are also saved as a Markdown file per conversation in Application Support/Companion/Wrap-ups (the folder button in Notes opens it), with key points, decisions, action items, follow-ups and open questions
- Notes for later: when you stop listening (and whenever you press the wand in the Notes view) the conversation is written up as structured notes: a short summary, a section per topic, decisions, action items and open questions. They are saved with the conversation, refreshed after a recheck, and the save button exports the notes plus the cleaned transcript as a Markdown file
- Guided voice setup: read five short sentences aloud, one at a time, and it advances as it hears you
- Voice separation on the mic (optional): record your voice once, then in-person talks and speakerphone calls work too; your voice stays You, every other voice becomes P1, P2, ... and triggers suggestions. Three FluidAudio models work together. Live, the streaming Sortformer diarizer follows the continuous mic stream (primed with your recording), and each finished utterance also gets a voiceprint (WeSpeaker embedding) that is compared with your own voice and the voices heard so far: it overrules the diarizer when the voice clearly says otherwise and folds a voice the diarizer split in two back into one person. On Recheck, the offline pyannote pipeline (segmentation, embeddings, VBx clustering) re-labels the whole recorded session at once, which is the most accurate, and the live labels then follow its naming. Mic audio for this is kept in memory only (up to 30 minutes). Phone calls taken on the Mac (FaceTime, iPhone call relay) already arrive as system audio
- Reply suggestions streamed from your Farhelm gateway (local models with routing, cache and one ledger), Claude, OpenAI, Ollama, or More services (Gemini, OpenRouter, LM Studio or any OpenAI-compatible server), switchable anytime
- Screen answers: one hotkey takes a picture of what you are looking at and answers the question shown. It zooms in on the window under the mouse pointer and captures it at full resolution, instead of the whole monitor, so small text and diagrams keep their detail. The window itself is captured, so other windows overlapping it are left out. With Claude, OpenAI or Gemini as the engine the picture itself is sent along with the OCR text (Apple Vision, on-device), so diagrams, charts and figures are understood; the other engines get the text only. Optional auto-watch mode
- Screen watcher: with Screen on, Companion glances at the screen every few seconds (3 by default; 2, 3, 5 or 10 in Settings) with a tiny capture, reads it only when it changed and then held still, and answers only a question it has not answered yet; after a minute without change it slows to every 10 seconds
- Agent, from the chat: there is one chat box. A question is answered; a message that asks for something to be done on the Mac ("deploy the frontend", "what changed in this repo since yesterday", "write a prompt about the settings window in the Claude chat and send it") goes to the agent, by its wording or because the model hands it over (so "can you do it" about something on screen works). It works like a coding agent, in rounds: it proposes the next step, you allow it, it runs, and the result goes back to the model, until the task is done or it needs you
  - Tools: RUN a shell command and read its output (in your login shell, starting in the working folder from Settings; `cd` carries over), OPEN an app, CLICK a button, checkbox, link or menu item by its label (through the macOS Accessibility API; menu paths like `File > Export…` work), TYPE into the app in front, press a KEY or shortcut, WAIT. It clicks by label only, never by position
  - Self-check: each step's result is verified and recorded. An opened app must come to the front (one retry), a click names the control that was pressed (one retry if it was not found), typed text is looked for on screen afterwards (never retyped). A step that could not be confirmed is marked so in the chat and the model is told not to assume it worked
  - Screen in one step: every round the agent is given the app in front, its window title, the page address when it is a browser (asked from the browser; macOS asks once to allow it) and the window's text, so it never has to run osascript or screencapture to look. Screen answers get the same header
  - Consistency: what the agent has done, with results, and what is still waiting for approval is given to every other prompt as fact, so a reply never contradicts it. The follow-up after a task adds only what the report left out, plus next steps
  - Permissions: every step that changes something is shown first, with Allow, Always allow and Deny. With "Run read-only commands without asking" on (Settings > Agent, on by default), commands that only read (ls, cat, grep, git status, git diff, osascript queries, screencapture to /tmp ...) run without asking; writing, deleting, sending and typing always ask. Always allow remembers the program (or program and subcommand, such as `git commit`); the list is in Settings > Agent and each entry can be removed. A command that redirects output or uses a subshell is always asked about. Deny stops the task
  - Modes (`AgentAccess`, beside the model in the chat box: one switch, Manual or Auto, where the Auto side carries a mark for its kind (a pencil for edits, a bolt for full access) that changes it on a click, or one button with a menu when the chat box is narrow; kept per chat, with the start for new chats in Settings > Agent): **Manual** is the above. **Auto: edits** edits files and runs commands inside the chat's repository without asking, and still asks for anything outside it, installs and network tools (`curl`, `brew`, `npm install`, `git push` ...), `rm -r`, and control of other apps. **Auto: full access** asks about nothing. In every mode, reading or writing keys and credentials (`.ssh`, `.aws`, `.env`, keychains) is asked about, Stop works, and the step limit holds
  - The task, each command with the end of its output, and the outcome appear in the chat. Afterwards it says how it went and offers up to three next steps as buttons
  - Voice (on by default, Settings > Agent): "Companion, ..." gives it a task, and a short "allow" or "deny" answers its question. Stop ends it at any point; a command is ended after 2 minutes. A long task is not cut off: after a set number of steps (40 by default; 100, 250 or 500 in Settings > Agent) it stops and asks whether to carry on, and a yes gives it as many again. If it is stopped there, what it had done is handed to the next task in the chat, so "continue" picks up where it stopped
- Glass UI: a pulsing circle under the camera (click to expand, drag to move; position is remembered, the panel follows) and a panel with the live transcript plus two cards side by side: speech reply and screen answer, each streaming independently. Background style is Frosted glass or Clear with a darkness slider; text keeps full contrast at any transparency. The panel resizes from any edge (size remembered, cards grow with it) and follows the circle until you drag the panel yourself; from then on it stays where you put it, and Reset Overlay Position re-attaches it. Click-through mode (Cmd+Shift+T) lets you click and select behind the panel; Settings > Look > App > Reset brings everything back under the camera
- One conversation view: the transcript and the answers live in a single timeline, in the order things happened. What was heard is shown as compact lines (speaker badge plus text), what you typed or captured as a small note, and each answer as a card right after what it answers. Only the newest Reply and the newest Screen answer stay open; older answers fold to one line and open again on click. All / Reply / Screen chips filter it; each answer has copy, regenerate, reuse-prompt and delete on hover
- Fewer replies: by default a short remark that is not a question ("Okay.", "Simplify.") gets no automatic reply; anything of six words or more, and any question or request, does. Settings > Replies turns this off; the bolt button always drafts one. Only what the others say triggers a reply, never lines labeled You
- Times and matching: every line, typed note and answer shows its clock time. After Recheck has corrected the transcript it goes back over the suggested replies and marks each Matched or No match (does it answer something that was really asked?); the eraser removes the replies that match nothing along with the ones you did not say
- Used or not: a suggested reply is tagged Used, Partly used or Not used, judged by how much of it you went on to say aloud. The eraser button removes the automatic replies you did not say, so the saved conversation reads as it actually went, and passed-over suggestions are no longer fed back to the model as context
- Header mode switches: the sparkles icon turns the Reply thread on or off (drafting when they finish speaking) and the viewfinder icon turns the Screen thread on or off (answering new questions that appear on screen). The timeline header has a bolt button to draft a reply now and a capture button to answer the screen now
- Recheck the transcript: the Recheck button on the transcript (also in the menu) goes through the last 40 lines one at a time, from the newest back to the oldest, and redoes each against the lines around it: who said it, whether it holds two people's speech (then it is split), and how it should read (misheard words fixed, grammar and punctuation cleaned, written so it is easy to understand, meaning and facts unchanged). Each fix appears as soon as it is found, the line being checked is highlighted, and the run can be stopped at any point. The wand on a rewritten line shows what was originally heard, and Undo puts the whole transcript back. For a single wrong label, click the speaker badge to hand that line to the next speaker (You, Them, P1, P2, ...)
- Type to it: the input bar at the bottom sends a typed message with the transcript and earlier turns as context; the viewfinder toggle attaches a screen capture. The model switcher sits in the input bar (engine, then the Farhelm model or Auto, or the Claude model: the list is loaded from the Anthropic API for your key)
- Retrieval instead of truncation: your details, attached files and memories are cut into passages, and when they are too long to send whole, only the passages relevant to what is being asked go to the model (keyword match plus Apple's on-device sentence embeddings; this step never leaves the Mac). The opening of My Details is always sent. Each answer shows which sources it drew on
- Memory across conversations: when a conversation is written up, up to six facts worth knowing later are saved (who you spoke with, what you were asked and how you answered, commitments and dates) and the relevant ones are fed into later conversations. Settings > You shows every item, with delete and Forget everything, and the switch to turn it off. Stored only on this Mac (Application Support/Companion/memory.json)
- Prompt caching with Claude: the instructions and the always-sent part of your details are marked cacheable, so repeat requests within five minutes are processed faster and cheaper
- Context sized to the engine: every limit on prompt size comes from one place (`LLM/ContextBudget.swift`) and scales with the selected engine's context window and with how urgent the task is. Live replies stay small so they start fast (about 6k tokens on a local model, 10k hosted); typed and screen questions get more (14k local, 40k hosted) and longer answers; notes, recheck and reply verification use most of the window (up to 150k with Claude). For Farhelm and Ollama, set the window the model is loaded with in Settings > Engine (default 32k); Ollama is told to use it. Up to 1,500 transcript lines are kept per conversation
- Attach files: the paperclip in the input bar (or dropping files on it) adds PDFs, Word/RTF/HTML, text, code or images (via OCR) to the conversation; their text is context for every answer
- Answers stay put: when an answer already on screen is re-checked (the question grew, the screen changed a little), it stays exactly as it is with no "thinking" effect while the new version is written out of sight. If the new version says the same thing nothing changes; only a real difference replaces it
- Telling you from others, and learning: whether a mic line is you is decided by comparing its voice with your setup recording; the diarizer only numbers the other people. The verdict comes from a small per-user adapter (`VoiceHead`, a logistic classifier over voiceprints; the voice model itself stays frozen). It starts as "close enough to your recording" and is refitted in milliseconds every time you correct a speaker badge or run Recheck (whose by-voice verdicts are used as examples). Examples are kept between sessions in `voice-examples.plist`. Settings > Listening > Other voices > Add takes one voiceprint per person from a folder of public recordings (LibriSpeech dev and test sets live in `Data/`, which is not in git) so the adapter knows what other people sound like from the first session; your own corrections still outweigh them. Redoing voice setup resets what was learned about you, not the reference voices
- Speaker labels correct themselves: while listening, the accurate by-voice pass that Recheck uses runs by itself (first after 45 seconds, then whenever there are new lines, backing off on long sessions) and fixes who said what in place. Each newly verified line also teaches the live check. A badge you set by hand is never changed by any automatic pass. Toggle: Settings > Listening > Keep correcting while listening
- Settings live inside the panel (the gear, or Cmd+, from the menu), not in a separate window. Seven sections in the order you meet them: Engine, You, Listening, Replies, Screen, Agent, Look. Each is a card of rows with one line of explanation per setting; Done or Escape returns to the conversation
- The "+" in the chat box attaches files (documents, reports, code), images (their text is read) or a whole folder; dropping them on the box works too. A folder is read as its listing plus its smaller text files. Everything attached keeps its path, and the agent is told about it: it may read those files and, with your approval for each change, update them; its shell starts in the folder attached last
- Look: no drop shadows anywhere, the panel included; depth comes from the glass and its hairline edge. Corners are moderate (panel 20, cards 14, bubbles 12) and spacing is kept to what the content needs. The footer shows the five everyday shortcuts (Panel, Listen, Reply, Screen, Ask; Stop replaces Ask while the agent runs); the full list is in Settings > Keys. Cmd+Shift+L starts and stops listening from any app
- Panels: under the top bar every part of the window is a panel of its own, as in an editor: the sidebar, the chat, details, the browser, and in code the side bar, the editor and the terminal, each a rounded glass card (code, files and the terminal on a nearly solid surface) a 6pt gap apart and from the window's edges. In code the activity bar and the side bar it opens are one panel, apart from the editor but joined to what opens and closes them: the bar is a lighter strip down the panel's left side, the one showing sits in a small well that slides to the one clicked, and with the side bar put away the panel is just the strip. In code, with a folder open, Settings and Quit sit at the foot of that strip, as in an editor, rather than at the foot of the chats. The gaps are the dividers: three dots in the middle of each show where to take hold, light up in the primary under the pointer, and the whole edge glows while it is dragged; a double-click puts the size back. In the browser the page sits in its panel with rounded corners of its own, as in Arc. One modifier draws a panel (`panelCard(_:)`) and one view the grip (`DividerGrip`), both in `Shared/CompanionDesign/Panels.swift`; the iPad's chats and chat use the same, with the gap between them draggable
- Choice bubbles: choices the AI offers (the agent's question, the next steps after a reply, the ways to start a new chat) are bubbles (`ChoiceBubbles` in `Shared/CompanionDesign/ChoiceBubbles.swift`, on the Mac, iPhone, iPad and Watch alike). They rise in one after another out of a little blur with a light passing over each as it lands; under the pointer one lifts and glows and its edge turns slowly through the look's colors; the one picked fills with the primary, shows a check and sends a ring out while the others fall away, then it is acted on. With Reduce Motion they only fade. The turning edge redraws only while the pointer is on a bubble, so idle bubbles cost nothing. The pickers under the chat box (the model, the effort, Manual or Auto) wear the same look (`bubbleSurface(tint:lit:)`): the model in the primary, the effort neutral until a level is set, Auto in amber for edits and rose for full access, and the chosen side of Manual or Auto slides across in its own small bubble. The phone's pickers match.
- Liquid Glass: on macOS 26 and later the control layer (the top-right toolbar, the chip row, the chat box, the model picker, the Settings tab bar and segmented choices) uses the system Liquid Glass (`glassEffect`), through one helper in `UI/Theme.swift` (`liquidGlass(in:tint:interactive:)`). Content stays on plain surfaces. On macOS 14 and 15 the same shapes fall back to the tinted hairline surface
- Sidebar: the top bar shows the chat's name with a sidebar button in front of it. Hovering the button floats the conversations over the chat (a full-height glass card; it closes when you pick one or move away). Clicking it pins them in their own column and the window widens to the left so the chat keeps its size. It is a glass card in the app's own style: a title with a New button, a search field, then the projects and the conversations. A project opens and closes with a click: its chats (the supervisor's, each run's, any other started in it) are listed inside it, the newest few first with the rest on request, and "New chat" starts one in it; closed, it still shows when a task in one of its chats is running or waits on you. Rename, change folder and remove are in a project's menu (right-click); removing asks first and loses nothing: the project is remembered, its chats keep naming it and its board is kept, so adding its folder again brings it back as it was. A project that was lost before removal was made safe (off the list, its chats loose, its board still on disk) is shown in the list as "Off the list ... Click to bring it back", and one click puts it back as it was. Deleting a chat asks first too, and says when a task is running in it or it belongs to a board. Under the projects come the chats that are in no project, as cards under Today, Yesterday, This week and Earlier, the open one edged in purple (`UI/Sidebar.swift`)
- No menu bar icon and no Dock icon by default: Companion is only its floating panel. Settings > Look > App has "Show in menu bar" (a small menu: show or hide the panel, new conversation, settings, quit) and "Show in Dock" (click the icon to bring the panel back). Cmd+Shift+Space shows and hides it from anywhere, and opening the app again brings it back. Cmd+Q quits while the panel is in use (never from another app; it asks once if Companion is listening or the agent is working). Quit and Reset panel position are also in Settings > Look > App
- Shortcuts are editable: Settings > Keys lists every global shortcut. Click one, press the new keys (it needs Cmd, Ctrl or Option; Esc cancels), and it takes effect at once; a combination already in use is refused with the name of what uses it. Reset per shortcut or all at once. They live in `Support/Shortcuts.swift` (`ShortcutAction` is the list of actions, `Shortcuts` stores and registers them), and labels across the app read from there
- Effort: Auto, Low, Medium or High, in the model picker and in Settings > Engine. It applies to typed questions, screen answers and the agent; live replies and background work keep the engine default so they stay fast. Sent as `output_config.effort` to Claude (with extra output room, since thinking counts toward the limit), as `reasoning_effort` to OpenAI and Gemini, as a `Reasoning:` line in the instructions for gpt-oss through Farhelm (no extra request field, in case the gateway is strict), and as `think` to Ollama for gpt-oss
- The agent reports like an assistant: its turn shows the answer itself (what was found or changed, in prose or short Markdown), and above it one line that says what it took ("Ran 2 commands and opened 1 app"). Click the line for the steps, and a command for its output. While it works the line shows the current step. It runs commands in its own shell and never drives a terminal app by typing
- The agent asks and carries on: when a choice is yours, it stops with a question and two to four answers to pick from (or type your own in the chat box), then continues the same task with your answer instead of ending and waiting for a new instruction
- The agent's tools (`Support/AgentTools.swift`): besides the shell and the keyboard it has READ (a file with line numbers, or a line range), SEARCH (text in files), EDIT (replace one exact piece of a file; it must match once), WRITE (create a file), FETCH (a web page as text) and WEB (search the web, best effort through a public search page). Reads and searches follow the read-only-without-asking setting, except for keys and credentials, which always ask. In Manual, EDIT and WRITE always ask, and the card shows the change as red and green lines; the automatic modes run them on their own and they stay undoable from Changes. For tasks of more than three steps it keeps a checklist (PLAN) that is ticked off in the chat
- Rename a chat by double-clicking its name in the top bar, or with the pencil (or a right-click) on its row in the sidebar. A named chat keeps its name
- Context per chat: a small ring beside the chat's name fills as the conversation takes up more of the engine's context window, with the token count next to it; hover for the split between what was heard, answers, attached files and notes. Each row in the sidebar shows its chat's size too. Sizes are estimates from length (`ContextUse`)
- Usage and cost (`Support/Usage.swift`): every request to an engine is metered in one place (`LLM.stream`): what it was for, which model, tokens in and out, cached tokens, and how long it took, using the engine's own counts when it reports them (Claude, OpenAI, Ollama) and an estimate from text length otherwise. Kept in `usage.jsonl` for 90 days. Settings > Usage shows cost and tokens for a period, by purpose and by model, editable prices per model (Claude's list prices are built in; a local engine's price is your own running cost), a plan check (price, token allowance, your cost and margin at the measured rate) and a CSV export. A chat's cost so far is in the tooltip of its context ring. A request is filed under the work it was made for (`WorkScope`): its chat, and in a project the project and the board task, wherever that chat is when the request ends
- The conversation's tools (context ring, recheck, clean up, reply now, capture the screen) float at the top right of the conversation and show only while the pointer is over it, or while a recheck is running
- Settings layout: a list of sections down the left (Engine, Usage; You, Listening, Replies, Screen, Agent; Keys, Look) with a search box that also finds sections by the settings inside them, and the chosen section on the right. The panel widens for Settings and goes back afterwards. Usage has a monthly budget with a bar and a line saying whether the month is on track
- Chat reads like a document: what was heard or typed sits in bubbles on the right, and answers are plain text across the page with no box around them
- Placement (Settings > Look > App): "Keep on top" floats the panel above every window (on by default; off makes it an ordinary window other apps can cover), and "Show on all desktops" keeps it on every desktop and over full-screen apps (on by default; off leaves it on the desktop it is on)
- Content zoom: Cmd+Plus, Cmd+Minus and Cmd+0 make the content larger, smaller and the usual size again (70% to 200%; also in Settings > Look > Size). It applies to the conversation, to pages in the browser and to code in the editor
- Whole-panel columns: the browser and the workspace each have a button that gives them the whole panel; the chat steps aside (keeping its place) and comes back from the same button, from the chat button in the top bar, or when the column is closed
- Workspace (`UI/Workspace.swift`, the `</>` button in the top bar): a column beside the chat for the folder attached to the chat (or the agent's working folder). Files: a tree and a code editor with basic coloring; Cmd+S saves. Search: plain-text search across the folder's text files. Changes: the history of every file the agent edited or wrote and everything saved in the editor, each with a before/after comparison, Undo, and "Go back to this"; "Undo all" reverses a whole task. Layout, as in an editor: the files at the side (when there is room), what is being worked on in the middle (the open file, or a comparison), and a panel of tools along the bottom whose top edge drags up and down (Cmd+J hides it): Terminal, Search, Changes, Git, and Files when they have no room at the side. Terminal (`UI/Terminal.swift`, Ctrl+`) is a real terminal: your login shell on a pseudo-terminal, drawn by SwiftTerm (pinned to 1.20.0), so editors, `htop`, `ssh`, password prompts, colors, arrow keys and Ctrl+C work as in Terminal.app. One shell per folder, kept running while the panel is hidden; it follows the look and the content zoom, and the file list refreshes when the terminal is hidden or the shell ends. `UI/ANSI.swift` turns colored command output into styled text for places that show output without a terminal. Comparisons (`UI/DiffPane.swift`) open in the middle, before on the left and now on the right, level with each other, with a switch to one list of what changed: from a row in Changes, a file in Git, or an edited file under Used in this session in Details
- Advanced (`Advanced/`, `UI/Advanced/`, `Shared/CompanionBoard`; the stacked-squares button in the top bar, beside Chat and Code): a project run as a whole. The project's board has the middle and the chat is a column on its right, as the workspace is in code; with no project open the board asks for one. The chat is the project's supervisor (`Advanced/Supervisor.swift`): a model that plans the goal as tasks, says for each what done means, what it waits on and what it should take in tokens, and proposes a model for every task with its reason, choosing from the engines that are set up by where each runs, what it costs and how capable it is (`Advanced/ModelRoster.swift`; `ModelRouting` gives the same answer by rule when no model is asked). The supervisor only asks: it ends its answer with a block of changes and the board's rules decide (`Board.apply`). It cannot mark work done, move or remove a task with a run going on, replace a model you chose, or make tasks wait on each other in a circle, and what it could not do is said in the chat. The board is shown four ways: Board (Backlog, To do, In progress, Review, Done; drag a card to another column, or onto a card to sit above it), Table (every task a row with its numbers, grouped by status or phase, its status and model changed in the row), Timeline (when each task waited, was worked on and ended, with every run on its row) and Usage. A click on a task opens it beside the board: what to do, what done means, what it waits on, its model, its runs with what each cost, notes and history. Start makes a run, in the task's own chat in the project: its first run starts the chat, and every later run of the task (a retry, a redo after review, "continue") goes into the same chat, where the agent works on that task alone with the task's model (`AppState.startBoardRun`, `continueRun`). A run is an ordinary agent task and follows its chat's access level (the board's menu sets the level for new run chats), and the board follows the run: started, In progress; finished, Review; failed or stopped, To do. In a git repository each task works in a copy of the repository of its own, on a branch of its own (Give Each Task Its Own Branch, on by default in the board's menu): a git worktree made from the branch the project's folder is on, in `~/.companion/worktrees`, on `task/t-<n>-...` (`TaskCopies`, `WorkingCopy` in `Shared/CompanionBoard`). All its runs work there, so runs at once stay out of each other's way and out of your folder, and "Runs at Once" can be raised; the supervisor's yes ends at the copy's edge. Approved, its work is merged into the branch it came from as a merge commit, worked out by git without touching any folder, and your folder moves up to it only when no change of yours is in the way; a conflict sends the task back for a run to settle, and anything only you can settle leaves it in Done with Merge Now. The task's panel shows its branch and the files it changed (a click shows one beside how it was), Show in Finder, and Throw Away, which deletes the copy and its branch. Without git, runs work in the project's folder. Review gate (on by default): the supervisor checks a finished run's report, changed files, command outcomes and, on a branch of its own, the branch's diff against what done means; approved, the task is done (and its work merged), otherwise it goes back with notes the next run is given. Autopilot (off by default, a switch per project): starts ready tasks, most pressing first, only for a project whose board was opened since launch; it stops when the budget is used up and leaves a task whose last two runs did not finish for you. Budget: a limit in tokens, dollars or both; no run starts past it. Usage adds up every request made for the project per model, task, chat, kind of work and day, with a CSV to copy. Goals: a project has one goal at a time and keeps the ones before it. New goal (beside the goal at the top of the board, and in its menu) turns the board to a new goal; the goal before is kept with its tasks, as reached when all of them are done and otherwise as set aside, and its open tasks stay with it unless you bring them along. A click on the goal lists the earlier goals, each with its tasks, how far it got and what it cost; "Work on it again" goes back to one, and the goal left for it is kept in its turn (`UI/Advanced/GoalsPanel.swift`, `BoardGoal`). Recommendations: wherever you are asked to choose (a step that needs a yes, an agent's question with answers, a task waiting in Review with the gate off), the supervisor says which answer it recommends; that answer carries "Recommended" and the reason is given beside it. It is advice: you decide, and you can answer before it comes. Problems are grouped by file; a row opens the file at the line, selected and scrolled to, and the lines with errors or warnings are tinted in the editor. Show Problems is ⇧⌘M. Settings shows the link and a QR code to scan. It is dependency-free: a minimal HTTP/1.1 server on `Network.framework` serves one page, streams the conversation as it changes over Server-Sent Events, and takes a message back through the very `send` and `stopAgent` the chat box uses. A secret pairing code (in the link's `#`, kept in the Keychain so it survives restarts) is required for anything with data; New code in Settings replaces it and cuts every phone off. On iPhone the page installs from Safari with Add to Home Screen and runs full screen as an app (web manifest, touch icon from the app's own icon, code remembered on the phone). From the phone: send messages, tap the agent's offered answers, stop it, switch between recent chats or start a new one; code blocks and inline code are formatted; screen captures show as pictures. The link uses the Mac's Bonjour name (`name.local`), so a saved phone app keeps working when the Mac's IP changes (the IP link is shown too, for browsers that cannot look up `.local`). While a reply streams only the turns that changed are sent, not the whole chat, and a heartbeat every 15 seconds keeps the feed open and drops phones that have gone; approving what the agent wants to do on the Mac (Allow / Deny) stays on the Mac. macOS may ask once to accept incoming network connections.
- With the panel put away (`UI/OrbitDock.swift`): a glass status card rides with the orbit (below it, above it near the bottom of the screen): the chat's name and what the agent is doing, amber with its question when it waits for you, mint "Done" or rose "Stopped" with the first line of the answer for a few seconds after a task. It shows while there is something to say, and when the pointer is on the orbit or it is tapped; clicking the card opens the chat. Dragging the orbit moves it; a tap never opens the panel, but closes it when it is open. The card is gone whenever the panel is showing, so it never covers it. The orbit has no background of its own, and shows its mood: a slow turn when idle, busy while working, nearly still and amber while waiting on you, mint or rose for a few seconds after a task.
- Opening the panel never moves the orbit: the panel is placed on the first side of it with room (below, above, right, left). The orbit follows only when you drag the panel yourself, and steps aside only if the panel is resized or enlarged into it.
- The panel and the floating orbit travel together and never overlap (`OverlayController.keepIndicatorClear`). The orbit can sit anywhere: drag it and it stays where it is dropped while the panel stays put (dropped on the panel, it slides to the nearest clear place; with the panel away, the panel will open beside it). Drag the panel and the orbit moves with it, keeping its own place beside it; only if that would take it off the screen or onto the panel does it take the nearest free place (above, below, or level with the panel's top on either side). It glides there rather than jumping, trailing the panel slightly and settling softly (an eased step each frame, kept running while windows are dragged), with a 10 point margin. It never passes over the panel: if the panel grows into it, it is put clear at once, and changing sides it fades across instead of sliding through. Its side is kept unless another is much nearer, so small moves do not flip it. When the panel fills its screen the orbit is put away until there is room again.
- Fitting, not overlapping: the row under the chat box (model, effort, the agent's mode) is laid out with `ViewThatFits`, fullest form first, so in a narrow chat names are dropped before any control is pushed over another; the top bar lets go of the command-palette button below 640 points (its keys still work), and the panel is never narrower than 540. While Settings is open the list of conversations steps aside and Settings' own list of sections is the sidebar.
- Checkpoints (`AppState.restoreCheckpoint`, `ChangeStore.rewind`): every message is a checkpoint, the chat and its files as they were just before it. Where files have been changed since, "Restore checkpoint" shows above the message on hover. It asks first, then puts every file this chat changed since back as it was, removes that message and everything after it from the chat (so Details' outputs and what the session used go back too) and returns the message's words to the chat box. Each file put back is recorded in Changes, so a restore can be undone there, and in a git repository it is committed as a new "Companion: restore checkpoint ..." commit; history is never rewritten. What commands did outside of files is not undone.
- Context beyond the chat (`Support/ProjectContext.swift`): every agent task is given the repository's own instructions file, the first found of `COMPANION.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and `.github/copilot-instructions.md` (up to 8,000 characters), and a list of the skills on hand. A skill is a folder with a `SKILL.md` (a `name:` and `description:` at its top); only its name and one line go to the model, which reads the file when a task matches, so many skills cost little and several can be used in one task. Skills are looked for in `<repository>/.companion/skills`, `<repository>/.claude/skills`, Companion's own `skills` folder (the command "Open Skills Folder") and `~/.claude/skills`. Neither instructions nor skills change what needs approval. When the agent's history outgrows its room, the bulk of old results is dropped before any step is: what was done and how it went stays, and questions, the checklist and skills that were read are kept whole.
- Helpers (the agent's `DELEGATE:` step; `AppState.delegate`, `SuggestionEngine.helperStep`): a question handed to a second, smaller agent with a context of its own. It reads and searches (READ, SEARCH, FETCH, WEB) for up to eight rounds and only its report, at most about 3,000 characters, goes back into the task, so a long look through a codebase does not fill the main context. A helper cannot run commands, change files or use apps, is refused keys and credentials, and is given nothing about the user or the task beyond the question.
- History (`Support/History.swift`): `ChangeStore` keeps every changed file as it was before and after, by content hash, under Application Support/Companion/history, so undo works in any folder. Where the folder is a git repository, the same changes are also committed on the current branch as "Companion: <task>" (Settings > Agent > "Commit changes to git"; nothing is pushed). Changes a shell command makes are not tracked by Companion's own history
- The orbit mark (`UI/Orbit.swift`, design kept in `Design/`): Companion's mark is a ball of orbit lines around a soft source of light, drawn in the look's colors. It is the app icon (`Resources/AppIcon.icns`), the floating circle (drifting when idle, turning while listening, busy while working) and the empty chat. While the agent works, an orbit appears on screen where it clicks or opens something, and a small one rides the caret as it types; Settings > Agent > "Show the orbit while it works" turns that off
- Looks (`Support/Look.swift`, Settings > Look): a look is the panel's two colors plus its glass. Color: seven ready shades, or your own main and partner colors by picker or hex code (fitted to the glass so text stays readable). Glass: see-through and blur (five steps) for the whole panel, or separately for the top bar, the conversation and the chat box area, plus how solid the bubbles are. A look can be saved under a name and switched back to
- Live facts (`Support/LiveFacts.swift`): a question about the weather is answered from a lookup made just before the reply (wttr.in; the place named in the conversation, or the network's rough location when none is), and one about news, scores or prices from a quick web search. Every reply is also told never to state a live fact it was not given, and to say it could not check instead of inventing one. Lookups are cached for five minutes. With Settings > Screen > Privacy > "Use my location" on (`Support/Location.swift`, macOS asks once), the weather here and "where am I" use the Mac's real location; it is used for the answer and not stored
- Speaking to Companion: when there is no other party and you talk to it directly, a live reply answers you as the assistant, and a spoken request to do something on the Mac is handed to the agent (each action still asks first)
- Beside the chat (`UI/SidePanel.swift`): two buttons in the top bar open a column on the right. Details has no title of its own: its three sections each fold to their heading on a click (the heading then says what is inside, such as "3 of 7 done"), which is remembered, and the close button sits on the first heading's line. It shows Progress (the agent's checklist as a timeline, the step in hand marked), Outputs (files it wrote or changed: the newest as a preview card, a picture as itself and text by its first lines, the rest listed; click to show in Finder) and Used in this session, request after request, each a compact two-line card (what was asked and when; the model and a count of each kind of thing used) that opens on a click into the full rows: what it drew on and changed (the model that did the work, attachments, web pages shown by their sites' icons, files read, files edited, commands, memory). The globe opens a small built-in browser: type an address or words to search, and pages the agent fetched open there with a click. It is its own browser, sharing nothing with Safari or Chrome. It has tabs (`Support/Browser.swift`): the plus opens a new one, links that ask for a new window open in one, and a new tab shows Suggestions on the panel's own background: pages from this chat first, then pages visited before (kept in `browser-history.json`; right-click a card to remove it or clear them all). The panel widens to the right for the column
- Floating bars: the top bar and the chat box float over the conversation, which runs the full height of the panel and scrolls behind them. Each bar has a blur that fades toward the conversation, so content softens away under it. Their heights are measured, and the conversation leaves that much room at its ends, so the first and last entries can always be scrolled clear
- Attachments: while you write, what you attach shows inside the chat box above the text (a picture as a thumbnail, anything else as a small card with its name; hover for the cross). When the message is sent they leave the chat box: the message shows their names, and the files stay with the chat as context, listed under Used in this session in Details, where one can be found in Finder or removed
- Panel size: the panel is not resized to suit what opens in it. Opening Settings, the sidebar, details, the browser or the workspace leaves it the size it is, and the columns share the room it has, each narrowing down to its least (sidebar 170, chat 320, details 220, browser and workspace 320). Only when even those do not fit is the panel widened, and then by just enough that nothing is cut off. Its size changes only by hand: drag an edge, drag a divider between columns (`UI/ColumnDivider.swift`; double-click one to reset its column), press the green button or double-click the top bar to fill the screen. Column widths are remembered. In code the chat keeps a share of the width (a little over a third until its divider is dragged), so it grows with the panel instead of staying narrow beside a wide workspace. The same divider sits wherever two parts share a width, each remembered and reset by a double-click: the files beside the editor, the details beside the workspace, Before and Now in a comparison, and the list of pages in Settings
- One answer per question: an automatic reply covers everything they say until you speak, and is refreshed in place only when the question materially changes. When they add to a question that is already being answered, the new answer builds on the text already written (even a half-generated one) and the words on screen stay put until the answer actually differs. While the question still sounds unfinished the answer is tagged Draft; it is confirmed when the question ends with a question mark or a finished sentence, or after they pause. A screen that hasn't changed is not answered again; the existing turn is updated instead
- Conversations are saved automatically (Application Support/Companion/conversations, screenshots included), and can be reopened to continue or deleted from the history view; New Conversation is Cmd+Shift+N in the menu
- Read-along: as you speak the newest reply or screen answer, the turn dims what you've already said and scrolls itself, so you never scroll mid-call; while an answer streams in, the thread follows the stream too
- Invisible to screen sharing and recordings (sharingType = .none, toggleable)
- Non-activating windows: clicking Companion never steals focus from your call

### Subscriptions as an engine

Besides API keys and local models, Companion can answer through a subscription you already have (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini) by running the vendor's own command-line tool: Claude Code, Codex or Antigravity (Google's tool for personal Gemini plans). Choose it in Settings > Engine > Subscriptions, or in the model picker once a tool is found.

- Companion never sees the sign-in. Install the tool, run it once in Terminal and sign in there.
- The tool gets the conversation on standard input and runs read-only. In code mode it starts in the chat's repository and may read it; changes are still made by Companion's agent, which asks first.
- Adapters are data: `cli-agents.json` in Companion's Application Support folder. Add an entry (command, arguments, output format) to add another tool.
- Source: `Sources/Companion/LLM/CLIAgent.swift`.

### More services

Any service that speaks OpenAI's chat completions works through one adapter: Gemini (Google AI Studio), OpenRouter, LM Studio on this Mac, or Other for a server of your own (vLLM, llama.cpp's server, a company gateway). Choose More services in Settings > Engine, then the service, its key and its model; Refresh lists the models the service offers.

- Keys are kept in the Keychain, one per service. `GEMINI_API_KEY` and `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` work too.
- The address is the service's own unless you change it. For Other, give it up to `/v1`. An address on this Mac or your network counts as local: the context window setting applies, and the step limits for engines billed by use do not.
- The agent uses the service's tool calls. Pictures go to Gemini; the others get the screen's text.
- Services are data: `Sources/Companion/LLM/CompatibleServices.swift`. Adding one is a new entry there, not new code. OpenAI and Farhelm go through the same adapter and keep their own place in Settings.
- Ollama keeps its own client: its OpenAI-compatible route cannot set the context size, which Companion sets on every request.

## Using it

- Window buttons: the red, yellow and green dots at the top left close the panel (the circle or Cmd+Shift+Space brings it back), shrink it to a pill, and enlarge it to fill most of the screen (click again to go back)

Stopping clears the task at once, so a new one can always be started

Interview answers in the first person from your own details with a concrete example and writes the notes as questions asked, what went well and what to improve. ## Build and run

```bash
cd ~/Project/farhelm/apple
bash build.sh
```

This builds a release binary, bundles `dist/Companion.app`, ad-hoc signs it and opens it. Other commands: `bash build.sh app` (build only), `bash build.sh clean`. Always run the bundled app, not `swift run` (permissions need the app bundle).

The terminal's shader needs Xcode's Metal Toolchain, a component Xcode downloads and macOS mounts at a path that changes (after a restart, for one). `build.sh` looks after both ways that breaks a build at `Shaders.metal`: it asks Xcode for the component when Xcode says it is missing (`xcodebuild -downloadComponent MetalToolchain`; if that hangs, install it from Xcode > Settings > Components), and it drops a saved build plan that still names a mount which is gone ("unable to spawn process .../metal").

## iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch

`Shared/CompanionDesign` is Companion's design (colors, glass, bubbles, the working mark, the fire ball, the plan and the work trail), used by the Mac app and the mobile apps alike, so a change shows everywhere. `Mobile/` holds the native Companion apps: follow the chats on your Mac, write to them, answer the agent and stop it. The Watch shows the task's status and plan, the last reply, and Stop, and reaches the Mac through the iPhone.

As on the Mac, each chat keeps what is being written in it (`Mobile/App/Drafts.swift`): switching chats swaps the box's words, which outlive the app, and files picked for a message stay with their chat while the app runs. Picking a next step or an answer to the agent's question sends it without touching the box. A new chat offers Dictate, Type, Photo and Screen as bubbles. On iPad the chats and the chat are panels a gap apart, the gap draggable as on the Mac.

1. On the Mac: Companion > Settings > Remote, turn on **Let the Companion app connect**.
2. Open `Mobile/CompanionMobile.xcodeproj` in Xcode. Under the Companion and CompanionWatch targets > Signing & Capabilities, pick your team (a free Apple ID's Personal Team works). Pick your iPhone or iPad at the top and Run.
3. In the app, tap **Scan the code** and scan the code in Settings > Remote (the iPhone Camera app opens it too).

Away from your Wi-Fi, through a relay (`Shared/CompanionLink`, `Support/RemoteRelay.swift`): with **Reach this Mac from anywhere** on in Settings > Remote, the Mac keeps a connection out to a relay (the address comes from `CompanionRelayURL` in `Resources/Info.plist` and can be changed in Settings), and the pairing code also carries the relay and a fresh 32-byte key. The app tries the Mac's local name, its Wi-Fi address and its Tailscale address first, all at once; when none answers, it meets the Mac on the relay. Nothing on the Mac listens to the internet, and nothing needs installing on either side.

- Sealed end to end: every frame is ChaCha20-Poly1305 under a key HKDF-derived from the pairing key, one per direction, with the channel and the sender bound in. The relay opens the channel with a seat, also derived from the key but separately, so holding it reads nothing; the channel is named by the seat's hash, so its name (which lands in logs) opens nothing. Frames older than five minutes and repeats are refused, so the relay cannot replay a message. New code replaces the key with the pairing code.
- Same rules as at home: a request through the relay is answered by the same `handle` in `RemoteServer`, and the feed comes by subscription (renewed every 25 seconds, the Mac drops a device after 70). Approving what the agent does stays on the Mac.
- The relay is Farhelm's (`crates/farhelm-relay` in the Farhelm repository), which takes seat channels (`c1-…`, opened by the `X-Relay-Seat` header) without a Farhelm account; after updating it, `./deploy/redeploy.sh relay` in the Farhelm checkout puts it live. Any relay that forwards `{channel, sender_id, nonce, ciphertext}` text frames on `/v1/channel/{channel}` to the channel's other members and checks the seat works.
- Long messages (a photo, a big chat) go in 160 KiB frames; past 48 unacknowledged frames the sender waits for acks, so the relay never has to drop any.

Tailscale still works as before: its address is one the app tries directly.

With a free Apple ID, apps installed from Xcode stop opening after 7 days; run them from Xcode again to renew. `cd Mobile/CompanionKit && swift test` runs the shared code's tests, and `cd Shared/CompanionLink && swift test` the link's (its fixture is the one farhelm-relay's tests check too). `cd Shared/CompanionBoard && swift test` runs the board's rules (Advanced).

## How it works

```
Mic (AVAudioEngine)        System audio (ScreenCaptureKit)
        │                             │
   AudioResampler (16 kHz mono) ──────┤
        │                             │
   SpeechChunker (energy VAD, partials + finals)
        │
   Transcriber (FluidAudio Parakeet v3, actor)
        │
   AppState transcript (You / Them)
        │                      Screen (SCScreenshotManager + Vision OCR)
        └──────► SuggestionEngine ◄───┘
                     │
  Claude / OpenAI / Ollama / More services (streaming)
                     │
            Glass overlay suggestion card
```

- `Sources/Companion/UI/Theme.swift` holds `Theme.iconSymbol`, the placeholder icon in the circle. Swap that one constant when the final icon is decided.
- Companion's own windows are excluded from its screen captures, so screen answers never read Companion itself.
- Private details stay on the Mac (`Sources/Companion/Screen/Redaction.swift`): password managers are never captured, and password fields and card numbers are blacked out before any screen picture or its text reaches a model. The `privateApps` setting adds more apps by bundle identifier.
- The agent acts only where it planned to (`DeviceControl.leaseRefusal`): it never types into a password field, and a keyboard or press step is skipped and planned again when the cursor has moved to another field, or another window has come to the front, since the plan was made. Every step it takes is added to a permanent record, one JSON line per event, in `~/Library/Application Support/Companion/Actions/` (`ActionRecord.swift`), with long typed text, keys and tokens left out.
- Staying light while it works: on the Mac every SwiftUI update is a layout pass over the whole window, so anything that moves for as long as it is shown (the live step's dot, the listening dot, the level meter) runs in Core Animation (`Sources/Companion/UI/LiveLayers.swift`), never as a `repeatForever` animation. Values that change many times a second stay off `AppState` (the audio levels live in `AudioLevels`), a streaming answer reaches the screen at most every 60 ms and its parsed lines are kept, theme colors are worked out once per change of look or appearance, and the phone's feed rebuilds its snapshot only when something changed.

## Notes and limits

- Use headphones on calls. With speakers, the other side leaks into your mic and can show up as You.
- Multi-display: choose which monitor screen answers read in Settings > Screen. Automatic reads the monitor the mouse pointer is on. System audio is captured for the whole Mac regardless of monitor.
- Recording calls may require consent where you live; check before using it on real calls.
- Only `Transcriber.swift` touches the FluidAudio API. If a future FluidAudio release changes names, fix that one file against their README quickstart.

## Troubleshooting

- Permissions keep being asked: the app must be signed with a stable identity. build.sh picks your Apple Development or "Companion Dev" certificate automatically; with no certificate it falls back to ad-hoc signing, and macOS treats every rebuild as a new app. After switching to a real certificate, reset the stale grants once and re-grant: `tccutil reset ScreenCapture com.harshsah.companion && tccutil reset Microphone com.harshsah.companion`. Separately, macOS re-confirms screen recording after app updates and about monthly ("Continue to Allow"); that one is OS policy and is one click.
- No Them text: Screen Recording permission missing, or the call app plays through a device ScreenCaptureKit can't tap. Grant permission and relaunch.
- "Cannot reach Ollama": run `ollama serve`, then `ollama pull <model>`.
- Model download stuck: first run needs network access to huggingface.co.
